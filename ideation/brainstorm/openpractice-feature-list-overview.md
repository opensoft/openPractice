# openPractice Feature List Overview — Brainstorm

Status: brainstorm
Kind: reference
Summary: research across athenahealth, eClinicalWorks, and AdvancedMD — with NextGen Healthcare and Tebra/Kareo in the statistically tied expanded set — against the OpenEMR practice-management baseline yields fifteen feature domains where openPractice can match leader capability while differentiating on provenance-linked revenue cycle, consent-governed engagement delivered through the HealthLinc patient app with AI-assisted intake and presence-aware arrival, worked operations queues, open metric semantics, and a portable HIPAA control plane — a practice-operations platform built to beat incumbents on workflow, trust, and total cost while owning everything except clinical care.

## Possible feats

- **Beat OpenEMR where it is weakest** — denial management, pre-service estimates, unified patient statements, consent-aware outreach, identity-safe registration, governed automation, and implementation burden.
- **Match the leaders where it matters** — automated eligibility everywhere, claim scrubbing with payer-rule depth, self-scheduling within safe constraints, integrated payments with saved-method consent, multi-location administration, cloud or bench delivery.
- **Differentiate where nobody stands** — financial provenance as a queryable API from eligibility to refund, communication decision records for every send, versioned open metric semantics citable outside the product, compliance evidence generated by normal operation, and an EHR boundary that is a versioned contract instead of a coupling.

## Motivation

OpenEMR proves open-source practice software can carry real billing and scheduling loads globally, but its live claims ride clearinghouse assembly, denials are rudimentary, estimates are thin, tasking hides in modules, analytics demand SQL, and hardening stays the operator's job. The commercial leaders define the preferred standard — athenahealth's services-led RCM network, eClinicalWorks' breadth at scale, AdvancedMD's configurable financial depth, NextGen's enterprise governance, Tebra's independent-practice simplicity — at subscription prices with opaque formulas and lock-in economics. openPractice exists in that gap: Frappe-native, independently usable with openChart or another EHR, original implementation, fully automated administration with humans confirming every consequential step.

## Goals

Observable outcomes rather than activity: a clinic books, clears financially, and confirms a visit without staff re-entry; every denial traces back through claim, charge, and source event in one query; every collected dollar traces to the estimate it fulfilled; no-show outreach respects consent evidence automatically; unattended work escalates before it harms cash flow; board metrics reproduce across releases with published formulas; auditors receive signed evidence packs without PHI exports.

## Non-goals

Not a fork or code copy of OpenEMR, Marley/Frappe Healthcare, or any incumbent. Not an EHR — signed clinical facts, encounters, documentation, orders, results, medications, and clinical decision support belong to openChart or another connected EHR. Not a billing bureau or collections agency. Not autonomous money movement — autopay runs under recorded authorization; refunds and material adjustments require human approval. Not hospital-inpatient revenue-cycle completeness at launch.

## What the system delivers

Fifteen domains organized as four clusters: a front-office-and-access funnel (identity-safe registration and HealthLinc app onboarding through AI-assisted previsit completion and presence-aware arrival), a provenance-linked revenue cycle (coverage certainty through patient receivables), operations-and-intelligence (worked queues plus reproducible metrics), and platform-and-trust (portable HIPAA control plane riding one governed integration spine). Each atomic document states the OpenEMR parity floor, the leader capability gaps with sources, and the proposed openPractice feature set with its twist.

## System model

```text
patients ──▶ FRONT OFFICE & ACCESS ──▶ REVENUE CYCLE ──▶ payers
             registration               coverage/auths
             scheduling                 charge/coding
             omnichannel comms          claims · remittance
             healthlinc app             patient billing        │
             ai intake · arrival              │                 │
                    │                          │                 │
                    └── OPERATIONS & INTELLIGENCE ───────────────┘
                     worked queues · open metric semantics
                                │
                    PLATFORM & TRUST
                     HIPAA control plane · FHIR/X12 spine · bounded automation
                     ▲ contract boundary: clinical facts stay authoritative in openChart
```

Value flows left to right (access feeds billing events, events reach payers); operations and intelligence read and write every surface through governed contracts; platform and trust underlie all four clusters. Clinical truth enters only as versioned FHIR R4 references consumed for administrative derivation, never edited here.

## How it fits

openPractice's ratified constitution (.specify/memory/constitution.md v1.0.0) already mandates HIPAA-by-design, security review gates, test-first development, clinical-boundary discipline, versioned integration contracts, and deployment portability — this feature list extends those principles into concrete competitive scope rather than inventing new governance. Enterprise patterns inform targets; no incumbent implementation code is consulted or copied. Frappe's DocType/workflow backbone supplies the extension path incumbents gatekeep, and the ideation lifecycle routes these packets toward OpenSpec proposals when staged.

## Key decisions and open questions

Load-bearing tensions preserved across the packet: connector-neutral claim state machines vs first-party clearinghouse operation; rules-first intelligence vs model-based features facing cold starts; self-service depth vs equity for patients without devices or documents; audit richness vs PHI sprawl in logs; portability floor vs optional managed conveniences. Each domain document carries its own open questions; none are settled here.
