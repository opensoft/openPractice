# Paper Prototypes: The Three Pages

Status: brainstorm
Kind: prototype
Summary: Three printed one-pagers — the morning huddle, the end-of-day close sheet, and the owner's weekly money page — drawn for a fictional four-staff family practice on Tuesday 2026-08-25 with one coherent set of numbers that reconciles across all three, followed by a composition verdict that finds the six-noun model survives contact with paper but forces four amendments: promises must admit an observed provenance, threads must carry a clock and not only an age, threads must be minted at intent rather than at arrival, and the party register must be named as substrate the six nouns presuppose.
Topics: openpractice-feat-review, paper-prototypes
Captured: 2026-08-27

## Page 1 — The Morning Huddle Page

### Design intent

Readiness is the sort order, not a column. The page opens with the ten people
who need a human before they arrive, in the order they walk in, each with one
sentence of what is missing and one sentence of what fixes it — never a
status name, never a percentage. The fourteen who are fine get four lines at
the bottom, because their value is being *countable*, not readable. Coverage
comes first because a page that tells you who is not ready without telling
you who is here is a trap. Everything below the schedule is either yesterday
refusing to leave, or a date that will hurt if nobody looks at it. The page
must be readable aloud in under four minutes and must fit one sheet.

### Where every section comes from

| Section | Noun(s) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| "Yesterday closed at 5:40pm. No days are open behind us." | **The Day** + **Close** | The previous Day's close record, rendered as one line. An unclosed day would appear here as the first item on the page. |
| Who is here today (people, hours, locations) | **The Roster** ∩ **The Day** | Coverage is a fact about a day about a roster member. "Out sick, called 6:52am" is an unplanned absence written onto today. Marcy is a Roster entry with bounded access (FD-1). |
| "Tomas is alone at the front desk 12–5" | **The Roster** ∩ **The Day** | Derived: the intersection of three people's hours on this date. Nobody typed it. |
| Open slots (Osei 3:15/4:15, Rosa 11:30/3:30) | **The Roster** ∩ **The Day**, minus **Threads** | Roster working time on this Day with no Thread on it. *See strain S-1 — the model does not currently say this out loud.* |
| "24 on the books · 10 need something" | **Threads** counted, **Readiness** as the sort | Requires the Thread to exist at booking, not at arrival. *See strain S-3.* |
| Ames — Rosa not approved with Ambetter | **Promise** (participation, effective date) → **Readiness** → **Thread** | The absent Promise fails readiness; the fix moves the Thread to a provider for whom the Promise is true. |
| Pruitt — plan ended Aug 1 | **Promise** (coverage, dated) → **Readiness** | The Promise expired; readiness is the diff. |
| Castellanos — interpreter not booked | **Readiness** over the Thread's requirements | The interpreter vendor is a counterparty, not a Roster entry; the booking becomes an action on the Thread. |
| Ruiz — nothing filled in, number may bounce | **Readiness** + *identity facts* | "Nothing filled in" is readiness. "The number may bounce" is a contact fact that belongs to no noun. *See strain S-6.* |
| Vance — owes him a written estimate | **Promise** not yet made (FD-4 GFE) → **Readiness** | A GFE is a Promise the practice makes; readiness says it is missing. |
| Frank — faxed referral never matched | **Thread** (non-visit: unmatched referral) → **Readiness** on the visit Thread | The Pile item is already a Thread; readiness on the appointment names it. |
| Hollis — owes $214 from June | **Thread** at a station, with dollars and an owner | A patient balance is a Thread, surfaced on the day the patient is in front of us. |
| Fenn — auth unanswered since Aug 19 | **Promise** (pending, with an age) → **Readiness** | The escape hatch ("see him for the visit only") is the FD-6.9 rule: hold the claim, never the patient. |
| Frisch — three texts, no reply | **Thread** (unconfirmed) → **Readiness** | The offer-the-slot-out fallback consumes the readiness verdict. |
| Okafor — Medicaid renewal unverified | **Promise** (coverage, unknown) → **Readiness** | "Unknown" is a legal readiness state, distinct from "false." |
| Ready — nothing to do (14) | **Readiness**, negative form | Their only job is to be counted, so the count is trusted. |
| Left over from Monday | **The Day** (carried by yesterday's Close) + **Threads** with owners | The two unsigned notes are Threads at the waiting-on-clinical station. The $173.00 is a **Money Line** that has not landed. |
| Clocks running out | **Promises** with dates + a derived clock | Filing limit, auth expiry, CLIA certificate, GFE due today. *The "days left" figure belongs to no noun as written — see strain S-2.* |
| The $2,310.00 held-claim row | **Threads** at one station + the **Promise** that releases them | This is FD's enrollment-gated hold with its retro-release. |

## Page 2 — The End-of-Day Close Sheet

### Design intent

The sheet is a subtraction, not a report: 24 booked, and every one of them
must end the page accounted for. Then 23 arrivals, and every one must have
charges or a person's name against a reason. Then the money, counted three
ways, with the difference shown *before* it was resolved so the sheet proves
the check happened rather than asserting it. The Pile section exists because
paper arriving is the day's other inbox and it also closes. Tomorrow's
blockers are created here, at the only moment anyone will do it. The
signature is not ceremony: the sheet is the compliance evidence, and it is
evidence precisely because it had to be produced anyway. One sheet, signed,
in a folder — that is the audit binder, accumulated a day at a time.

## Page 3 — The Owner's Money Page

### Design intent

Three questions in the owner's order — did it come in, did they pay what
they usually pay, where is it stuck — then one section that is only for him
and one line that puts the week in context. No status vocabulary reaches
this page: not "unapplied cash" but "money in, we don't know whose"; not
"PLB takeback" but "Medicare's wire was $212.40 less than the claims on it."
Every row is a number and a list behind it. The section he is meant to act
on holds exactly three items, each with a dollar figure and one action, and
two of the three actions are somebody else's to perform — he only has to not
stop them. Ten minutes, top to bottom, one sheet.

## Composition Verdict

### 1. Did every line derive from the six nouns without inventing a seventh?

**No line on any of the three pages required a seventh noun.** Seven printed
lines required something the six nouns do not currently *carry*, which is a
different and smaller problem than a missing noun. Listed exactly:

| # | The printed line | What it needed | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | P1: "Open slots: Dr. Osei 3:15 and 4:15 · Rosa 11:30 and 3:30" | capacity — Roster time on a Day with no Thread on it | **No new noun.** Clarification C-1. |
| 2 | P1: "Dr. Paul Whitcomb — Pine Ridge, 8–12" (the satellite's hours) | a location's operating hours, which belong to the practice, not to a person | **No new noun.** Clarification C-1: hours reach the page through Roster entries; the location is an attribute, not an object. |

### 2. Where the model strained

**S-1 — Capacity has no stated home.** An empty 3:15 slot is not a Thread
(no visit), not a Promise, not Readiness, not a Money Line, not a Day. It is
Roster time with nothing on it. The model text does say the Roster holds
"what job, at which locations, from when to when," so the data is there — but
nothing in the model says that scheduling capacity is *derived from the
Roster*, and a reader building a scheduler would reasonably invent a seventh
noun called the Schedule. This is the strain most likely to become a real
seventh noun by accident.

**S-2 — The Thread carries an age but every page displays a clock.** The
model gives the Thread exactly four properties: station, age, dollars, owner.
All three pages lean on *time remaining*: 16 days to a filing limit, 8 days
to an auth expiry, 53 days before Ambetter stops accepting a held claim, 35
days to a CLIA renewal, "decide by Sep 24." The review documents said this
themselves — F10's "show days remaining, not days elapsed" — and the noun
model then shipped only elapsed. Age answers "is this rotting?"; the clock
answers "when does this die?" They sort work differently, and the huddle page
sorts by the clock while the money page sorts by dollars.

**S-3 — "One per visit" cannot support Page 1 or Page 2's first block.**
Page 1's entire sort order attaches readiness to appointments that have not
happened. Page 2 disposes two no-shows and two cancellations, and a
disposition needs something to be a disposition *of*. If the Thread is minted
at arrival, neither block has an object. FD-7 already lists "when a new one
is minted" as a question the Thread contract must answer; the paper answers
it, and the answer is *not* "at the visit."

**S-4 — The non-visit Thread enumeration is short by at least two types.**
The model enumerates credit balance, unmatched referral, payer records
request, recoupment case, migrated balance. Page 2's Pile produced an
unmatched-but-matchable fax (resolved same day), a misdirected fax that is
not our patient at all, and a scanned insurance card. The first and third are
inbound items that become part of an existing Thread; the second must be
dispositioned, evidenced (notified and destroyed), and never joined to
anything. As written it is not a Thread, so it has no station, no age, no
owner — which means it is exactly the kind of thing that gets lost.

**S-5 — The Money Line is defined as "the externally verifiable fact," and
half the money page is about money that is not yet verifiable.** The model's
own text widens it — "a line on the bank statement, a check, a card batch, a
deposit" — a card batch is a Money Line the moment the terminal closes, three
days before the bank sees it. The definition sentence and the enumeration
disagree, and the money-day predictor lives in the gap.

**S-6 — The Patient is a surface with no noun, and identity facts belong
nowhere.** Six nouns, six surfaces, and "The Patient" is one of the surfaces
but not one of the nouns. That is *correct* as far as it goes — the patient
page is an index across Threads, Promises, and Money Lines. But the test the
synthesis proposes ("which of the six nouns does it touch?") returns nothing
for Hector Ruiz's phone number, for a household, for a duplicate merge, for a
guardian, for a preferred language, for a minor turning eighteen. Four
beta-blocking feats in the missing-feat catalog are identity feats, and the
model as written cannot classify any of them.

**S-7 — The Promise cannot express promise memory, which FD-2 made
primary.** "A dated assertion with an owner" describes a contract, an
authorization, a consent, an estimate we gave. It does not describe "Aetna's
median allowed for 99214 is $103.40, n=23, IQR $99.10–$107.80." Nobody
asserted it; there is no maker; and it is not dated so much as *windowed*.
This is the single most consequential strain because FD-2 put the learned
baseline on the critical path and the entire second section of the owner's
money page is built on it.

**S-8 — "Owner" does double duty.** On a Thread, the owner is *who must act*
(Dana, Tomas, Marcy — always a Roster entry). On a Promise, the model's
owner is *who asserted it* (Aetna, the state licensing board, the patient —
almost never a Roster entry). One word, two relations, and they collide the
first time someone writes "show me everything Aetna owns."

**S-9 — A surface boundary that felt arbitrary, and one that did not.**
Arbitrary: Page 3 §3's "Waiting on a note to be signed" row is money stuck,
which puts it on The Money; but the action is Dana asking Dr. Whitcomb, which
puts it on Today. Both are right, so the boundary between Today and The Money
is a *time horizon*, not a subject — Today holds what is actionable today,
The Money holds the same threads rolled up when they are not. That is worth
stating, because otherwise the two surfaces will be built twice. **Not
arbitrary:** the boundary between The Pile and The Thread held perfectly.
Every one of the six pages that arrived Tuesday became a Thread on arrival
and left The Pile the moment it had a disposition; The Pile is a station, not
a container.

### 3. Amendments forced

**Four amendments. Three clarifications. One naming fix.**

**A-1 — The Promise admits two provenances: declared and observed.**
A declared promise has a maker and a document (a contract, an authorization,
a consent, a GFE we issued). An observed promise has no maker, a sample size,
and a spread; it is what this payer has actually paid us. Both are dated
assertions with an owner; only the evidence differs. *Why this is the right
shape:* it makes FD-2's headline alert — declared and observed disagree —
expressible as one Promise with two sources in conflict, rather than as a
comparison between a Promise and something the model cannot name. Without it,
promise memory is a seventh noun in all but name.

**A-2 — The Thread carries a clock, not only an age.**
Add a fifth property: time remaining against the governing deadline, where
the deadline is derived from a Promise plus a date the Thread already holds
(date of service plus the payer's filing window; the auth's expiry; the
statement cycle; the 30 days on a demand letter). Age is retained; it answers
a different question. *Why forced:* every one of the three pages displays
days-remaining, and the review documents independently demanded it, and the
noun list does not carry it. A model that cannot express "16 days left" cannot
produce the huddle page's last section.

**A-3 — The Thread is minted at intent, not at arrival.**
A booking mints a Thread. So does an inbound referral, a phone call that
creates an obligation, and a waitlist entry. Cancellation, reschedule, and
no-show are *dispositions* — station changes with an owner and a date — never
deletions. *Why forced:* Page 1 has no object to sort by readiness without
it, and Page 2 has nothing to dispose. It also pays for itself twice: a
waitlist entry becomes a Thread at a station rather than a seventh object,
and reschedule-as-carry-forward (a named beta-blocking feat) becomes a
station change instead of a cancel-plus-create that orphans four artifacts.

**A-4 — Name the party register as substrate the six nouns presuppose.**
The six nouns are the *work* model. They refer to parties — patients and
households, payers, referring offices, vendors, the bank, the processor — and
those parties are not nouns in the model and should not become one. State
this explicitly, and amend the acceptance test accordingly: a feature must
answer *which of the six nouns does it touch* **or** *which party fact does
it establish*. *Why forced:* without it, four beta-blocking identity feats
(patient merge aftermath, household coordination, minor-to-adult transition,
interpreter and accommodation) fail the model's own test, and a reader will
conclude either that the model rejects them or that a seventh noun is needed.
Neither is true.

**C-1 — Capacity is a rendering of the Roster.**
State that scheduling capacity is Roster entries (person × location ×
working hours) projected onto a Day, and that an open slot is Roster time
with no Thread on it. No new noun; but say it, or a Schedule noun will be
invented by the first person to build the booking surface.

**C-2 — Every Pile page is a Thread from the moment it lands.**
Dollars may be zero; a station, an age, and an owner are mandatory from
arrival. Extend the non-visit enumeration with *inbound item, unmatched* and
*inbound item, not ours* (the misdirected-PHI case, which has a required
disposition and is not "trash").

**C-3 — A Money Line exists before it lands.**
Three states: expected, landed, explained. Only *landed* is externally
verifiable; only *unexplained-and-landed* is red. The money-day predictor is
the sum of expected Money Lines, which makes it arithmetic rather than a
forecast — and that is the difference between a number the owner trusts and
one he does not.

**N-1 — On a Promise, the party is the *maker*; on a Thread, the *owner* is
who must act.** One word currently carries both relations. The Thread's owner
is always a Roster entry; a Promise's maker usually is not.

**Explicitly ruled: no amendment needed, and here is why.**

- **Close is not a seventh concept.** Closing a Day is Readiness evaluated on
  the Day: what a closed day requires, minus what is true right now. Page 2
  is literally a readiness list for one Day, rendered to paper and signed. No
  amendment; state the identity, because it deletes the need for a separate
  close-checklist model and makes the close sheet fall out of machinery that
  already exists.
- **The three owner problems on Page 3 are a sort, not an object.** Each is a
  Thread-set or a changed Promise, ranked by dollars. No Alert noun, no
  Insight noun.
- **Counterparties are not Roster entries.** The interpreter line, the
  referring office, the bank, Ambetter's enrollment department — these appear
  as Promise makers, Money Line sources, and Thread attributes. The Roster is
  who works here. Marcy at Ledgerline *is* on the Roster because she does the
  practice's work under its authority (FD-1); the interpreter vendor is not.
- **Coverage and absence need no noun.** Kelly out sick is Roster ∩ Day. So
  is "Tomas is alone 12–5," which nobody typed.
- **Patient-level statistics need no noun.** "Third missed visit in a year"
  is an aggregate over that patient's Threads.
- **The cash drawer needs no noun.** Float, count, and over/short are Money
  Line; who counted is Roster.
- **Deadlines themselves need no noun.** Filing limits, auth expiry, the GFE
  dispute window, the CLIA certificate, the 30 days on a demand letter are
  all Promises with dates. Only the *derived clock* was missing, which is A-2.
- **The satellite location needs no noun.** It is an attribute on Roster
  entries and on Threads (place of service, which the Promise lookup already
  keys on).

### 5. Verdict

**PASS-WITH-AMENDMENTS.**

The six nouns carried 100% of the printed content with no seventh noun
required, and they did it while three pages written for three different
people at three different hours stayed numerically consistent with each
other. Two results in particular are stronger than expected. First, **Close
turned out to be Readiness applied to the Day** — Page 2 is not a new kind of
document, it is the huddle page's computation run against a Day instead of
against an appointment — which means the verb is not a seventh concept and
the close checklist does not need to be authored, only rendered. Second,
**The Pile held perfectly as a station rather than a container**: every page
that arrived Tuesday became a Thread on arrival and left the Pile the moment
it had a disposition, which is exactly the behaviour the model predicted and
is the hardest thing in the packet to get right.

The four amendments are all small and all forced by paper rather than by
theory. A-1 (observed promises) and A-2 (a clock, not only an age) are the
two that must land before anything else is written: FD-2 put promise memory
on the critical path and the model cannot currently express it, and every one
of the three pages displays a countdown the Thread cannot produce. A-3
(mint at intent) is cheap now and expensive later — it is a decision about
when a row is created, and FD-7 already scheduled it. A-4 (the party register
as substrate) costs one paragraph and rescues four beta-blocking feats from
failing the model's own acceptance test.

The one finding that should change how the Wave 2 rewrites are read: **the
model is a work model, not a domain model, and it must say so.** Every strain
found here is the same strain — the six nouns describe what is *happening to*
things (a thread moves, a promise expires, a day closes, money lands) and are
silent about the things themselves (who the patient is, where the office is,
what a slot is, what a code costs). That silence is a virtue as long as it is
declared. Undeclared, it will be read as a prohibition, and the first person
who needs a household or a schedule template will either invent a seventh
noun or conclude the model is wrong. It is neither.
